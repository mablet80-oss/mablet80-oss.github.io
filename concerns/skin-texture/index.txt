22:T1879,<h2 id="skin-quality-has-an-actual-definition">"Skin quality" has an actual definition</h2>
<p>It sounds like a marketing phrase, and for once it is not.</p>
<p>In 2021, a <strong>modified Delphi consensus</strong> of ten dermatologists and aesthetic physicians from <strong>eight
countries</strong> — including South Korea — defined good skin quality as <em>"skin that is healthy,
undamaged, and youthful in appearance"</em>, and broke it into <strong>four emergent perceptual categories</strong>:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Category</th>
<th>What it means</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>Tone evenness</strong></td>
<td>Uniform pigmentation, evenly distributed</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Surface evenness</strong></td>
<td>Freedom from visible imperfections — pores, wrinkles, scars, clarity</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Firmness</strong></td>
<td>Elasticity, tautness, hydration</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Glow</strong></td>
<td>Radiance, luminosity, brightness, complexion</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The panel found these held across Asian Pacific, European, Middle Eastern, African, Latin American
and North American assessments. They are not a Western framework applied to Korean skin.</p>
<h2 id="why-this-is-the-most-useful-page-to-read-before-a-consultation">Why this is the most useful page to read before a consultation</h2>
<p>Because almost every treatment sold to a visitor is described as improving "skin", and each one
actually moves <strong>one or two</strong> of those four.</p>
<p>A <strong>2025 consensus algorithm</strong> from a twelve-expert panel maps them:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Firmness</strong> — microfocused ultrasound, dilute calcium hydroxylapatite, ablative fractional lasers</li>
<li><strong>Surface evenness</strong> — fractional lasers, radiofrequency microneedling, biostimulatory fillers,
microtoxin, hyaluronic acid fillers</li>
<li><strong>Tone evenness</strong> — Q-switched and picosecond lasers, intense pulsed light, chemical peels,
vascular laser</li>
<li><strong>Glow</strong> — hyaluronic acid fillers, microtoxin, polynucleotides</li>
</ul>
<p>Read down that list and you can see the mismatch that produces most disappointment. Someone unhappy
about <strong>uneven tone</strong> is sold a booster that sits in the <strong>glow</strong> column. Someone who wants <strong>glass
skin</strong> — which is surface evenness plus glow — is sold a single tightening device, which is neither.</p>
<p>The treatment was not bad. It was aimed elsewhere.</p>
<h2 id="use-the-four-categories-as-your-script">Use the four categories as your script</h2>
<p>You do not need to know the devices. You need to be able to say which category you want, and to make
the clinic connect its proposal to it.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Pick your category, in your own words.</strong> "My tone is uneven." "My skin is rough close up." "It
has gone slack." "It looks dull."</li>
<li><strong>Ask which category each proposed item addresses.</strong> One item, one answer.</li>
<li><strong>If a proposal covers a category you did not name</strong>, ask why it is in the plan.</li>
<li><strong>If two items address the same category</strong>, ask what the second adds.</li>
</ol>
<p>That conversation takes five minutes and is worth more than any brand comparison.</p>
<h2 id="combination-honestly">Combination, honestly</h2>
<p>The 2025 panel's position is that <strong>combination treatment is becoming the standard of care</strong>, chosen
for complementary mechanisms across different tissue layers.</p>
<p>That is a real clinical view, and it is also the sentence a clinic will reach for when selling you a
bundle. The difference between the two is whether each item is justified <strong>by category</strong>. A
combination assembled after an assessment is a plan. A package priced before one is a product.</p>
<p>Two cautions on how much weight to give the algorithm itself:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>It is expert consensus, not trial data.</strong> Polynucleotides sit in the glow column while their own
evidence base is nine studies of 219 patients, rated low to moderate quality.</li>
<li><strong>A device does not carry equal evidence across every column it appears in.</strong> The 41-study review
of microneedle radiofrequency rates its evidence strongest for acne scars and striae, moderate for
periorbital wrinkles and rosacea, and <strong>weak for melasma</strong> — while the device is marketed across
all of them.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-the-same-consensus-says-about-the-unglamorous-half">What the same consensus says about the unglamorous half</h2>
<p>Worth quoting because it is the part omitted from a treatment quote.</p>
<p>The panel recommends <strong>pre- and post-treatment care to maximise results</strong>, tailored topicals
including <strong>ceramides, niacinamide and vitamin products</strong>, and <strong>diligent sun avoidance and
sunscreen use</strong>, particularly after a procedure.</p>
<p>If you are flying in for treatment, that is the half you take home, and it is the half that decides
whether the result holds.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-ask-at-a-consultation">What to ask at a consultation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Which of the four categories are we treating?</strong> Say the words; they are standard vocabulary.</li>
<li>What does each proposed item do, <strong>specifically</strong>, for that category?</li>
<li>If several items are proposed, what does each add that the others do not?</li>
<li><strong>What topicals should I be on</strong>, before and after, and can I get them at home?</li>
<li>What should I expect at four weeks, at three months, and when should I judge it?</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
