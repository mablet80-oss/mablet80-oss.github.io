1d:T1221,<h2 id="what-an-acne-scar-actually-is">What an acne scar actually is</h2>
<p>This decides everything else, and it is where most treatment plans go wrong.</p>
<p>An <strong>atrophic acne scar</strong> is not discolouration on the surface. It is missing or disorganised tissue
in the dermis — a structural defect with a shape. The flat brown or red marks left after a spot are
a different thing entirely: post-inflammatory pigmentation, which fades on its own and responds to
different treatment.</p>
<p>So the first useful question at a consultation is which of those you have, and in what proportion.
Treating pigmentation with a remodelling device, or scarring with a toning laser, is a common and
expensive mismatch.</p>
<h2 id="what-south-korean-clinics-offer">What South Korean clinics offer</h2>
<p>Four categories, doing different jobs:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Microneedle radiofrequency</strong> — needles carry energy into the dermis to provoke remodelling.</li>
<li><strong>Fractional laser</strong> — columns of thermal injury through the skin, prompting the same repair.</li>
<li><strong>Subcision</strong> — releasing the fibrous tethers pulling a rolling scar down. A procedure, not a
device.</li>
<li><strong>Filler</strong> — lifting an individual depressed scar, which is a temporary correction rather than a
structural one.</li>
</ul>
<p>Most plans combine them, and that is reasonable. What is not reasonable is a package quoted before
anyone has looked at what kind of scars you have.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-evidence-supports-best">What the evidence supports best</h2>
<p><strong>Microneedle radiofrequency</strong> has the strongest body of evidence among the options an international
patient will be offered.</p>
<p>A 2026 systematic review covering <strong>41 studies and over 1,000 patients</strong> found reductions in acne
scar scores of <strong>26–100%</strong>, with efficacy described as <strong>comparable to fractional lasers</strong>.</p>
<p>That range is wide and the width is honest: scar types respond differently, devices differ, and
settings differ. What it establishes is that the treatment works, not by how much it will work on
you.</p>
<h2 id="why-skin-type-changes-the-choice">Why skin type changes the choice</h2>
<p>This is the part that matters most for readers in East and Southeast Asia, and it is a better
argument than any brand claim.</p>
<p>The same review reports <strong>consistent safety across Fitzpatrick skin types</strong>, with post-inflammatory
hyperpigmentation in <strong>2.9–5%</strong> of cases, mild and transient adverse events, and no serious
complications across the included trials.</p>
<p>The reason is mechanical. Because the needles deliver energy <strong>past the epidermis</strong> rather than
through it, surface pigment is comparatively spared — which sidesteps the central risk of ablative
and high-fluence laser treatment in skin that pigments easily.</p>
<p>When efficacy is comparable and the risk profile is not, the risk profile should decide.</p>
<h2 id="what-results-are-realistic">What results are realistic</h2>
<p>Improvement, not removal.</p>
<p>The published outcomes are reductions in scar scores. Nobody in the literature reports clearance,
and a clinic promising it is making a claim the evidence does not carry. A realistic expectation is
that scars become markedly less visible, over a course, with remodelling continuing for months
after the last session.</p>
<p>Two practical consequences: judge the result late rather than early, and be sceptical of a package
sold with a promised endpoint.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-ask-at-a-consultation">What to ask at a consultation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Which of my marks are scars and which are pigmentation?</strong> Get the answer before the plan.</li>
<li><strong>What scar types do I have</strong>, and how does that change what you are proposing?</li>
<li>Which device, and what is its Korean product name?</li>
<li>What needle depth and energy, and why those for my skin type?</li>
<li>How many sessions, at what interval, and what is the total?</li>
<li>When should I judge the result?</li>
</ul>

<p>Atrophic acne scars are a structural problem, so the treatments that help are the ones that remodel
