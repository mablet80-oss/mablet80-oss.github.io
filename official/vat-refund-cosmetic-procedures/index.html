South Korea's VAT refund on cosmetic procedures ended on 1 January 2026

The 10% VAT refund for foreign patients having cosmetic procedures in South Korea applied to services supplied up to 31 December 2025. It was not extended.

Short answer

Can foreign patients still get a VAT refund on cosmetic procedures in South Korea?

No. The refund was a special measure under Article 107-3 of South Korea's Restriction of Special Taxation Act, and that article limited it to cosmetic medical services supplied on or before 31 December 2025. The 2025 tax reform did not extend it, so procedures from 1 January 2026 carry the full 10% VAT with no refund. Several official and clinic pages have not been updated and still describe the refund as available.

What the rule was, and what changed

Between 1 April 2016 and 31 December 2025, a foreign tourist who paid for cosmetic medical services in South Korea could reclaim the 10% value-added tax included in the price. The mechanism was not a general tourist tax refund. It was a specific measure written into Article 107-3 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act — a statute that exists to grant time-limited tax exceptions, each with an expiry date attached.

That expiry date is the whole story. The article limits the benefit to qualifying services supplied "up to 31 December 2025". The provision had been extended repeatedly since it was introduced; the 2025 tax reform did not extend it again. As displayed on the National Law Information Center on 8 September 2026, the statute was last amended on 23 December 2025 and took effect on 1 January 2026 with that end date intact.

The practical result is simple. A cosmetic procedure supplied in South Korea from 1 January 2026 carries 10% VAT, and there is nothing to reclaim on the way home.

Who this affected

The scheme covered foreign tourists receiving cosmetic medical services — the category that includes skin treatments, injectables, and aesthetic surgery. It did not apply to Korean nationals or to residents, and it did not apply to medical treatment that is VAT-exempt in the ordinary course, such as care for disease.

It also never applied at every clinic. A clinic could only issue a refund if it was registered to treat foreign patients and had a contract with a refund operator designated by the National Tax Service, with the operator's equipment installed. In practice that meant a patient had to ask in advance, and many clinics that advertised "tax refund" were quietly outside the scheme even while it ran.

Why you will still see the refund advertised

This is the part worth being careful about. The Korea Tourism Organization's own English page on the subject, dated 20 December 2023, still states that "a system for refunding value-added tax (VAT) on cosmetic surgery procedures for foreign tourists has been in effect since April 1, 2016." It has not been revised. Clinic pages, agency blogs, and English-language guides written before 2026 — or copied from those official pages — repeat it.

None of that changes the statute. When an official page and the law disagree, the law governs, and the law here is unusually easy to read: an article with an end date, and no amendment extending it.

How to check this yourself

The primary document is Article 107-3 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act on the National Law Information Center, linked in the sources below. Two things to look for on that page:

  • the qualifying period stated in the article — whether it still reads 31 December 2025, or has been amended to a later date by a subsequent tax reform; and
  • the amendment history at the top, which gives the act number and the date the current version took effect.

If a later amendment revives the benefit, that page will show it before any tourism or clinic site does. Korean tax provisions of this kind have been extended six times before, so a future reinstatement is not implausible — which is exactly why the primary source, and not a guide, is what you should check before you travel.

What to do about a quote that still includes it

If a clinic or an agency is quoting you a price "after tax refund" for a procedure in 2026, that quote is wrong, and the difference is 10% of the bill. Ask which refund operator they are contracted with and under what provision the refund would be paid. A clinic that cannot answer that has either not updated its pricing or is describing a discount it intends to fund itself — and those are very different things to be told before you commit.

The bottom line

The 10% VAT refund for foreign patients having cosmetic procedures in South Korea applied to services supplied on or before 31 December 2025 and was not extended. Procedures from 1 January 2026 carry the full VAT with no refund available. Budget from the quoted price, ignore any "tax refund" figure in an estimate, and check Article 107-3 directly if you want to know whether that has changed again.

Frequently asked questions

I had a procedure in South Korea in 2025. Can I still claim?
That depends on the refund operator's own deadline rather than on the statute, because the qualifying event is the date the service was supplied, not the date you claim. If your procedure was on or before 31 December 2025 and you kept the refund slip the clinic issued, contact the refund operator the clinic was contracted with. Do not rely on this page for the claim window — ask the operator directly.
Why does the Korea Tourism Organization page still say the refund exists?
That page is dated 20 December 2023 and has not been revised. It is evidence of how the scheme worked while it ran, not evidence that it still runs. The statute is the authority.
Does this mean cosmetic treatment in South Korea is now 10% more expensive?
For a foreign patient who previously claimed the refund, the effective cost rises by the refund they used to receive. The advertised price itself does not change, because Korean clinics quote prices with VAT included. What has gone is the money back afterwards.
Are non-cosmetic medical services affected?
No. Article 107-3 was a special rule for cosmetic medical services supplied to foreign tourists. Medical services that are exempt from VAT in the ordinary course, such as treatment of disease, were never part of this scheme.
A clinic is still promising me a tax refund. What should I do?
Ask them, in writing, which refund operator they are contracted with and under what legal provision the refund is being paid for a procedure in 2026. Treat an unclear answer as a reason to check the rest of their quote too.
