24:T11d6,<h2 id="what-the-rule-was-and-what-changed">What the rule was, and what changed</h2>
<p>Between 1 April 2016 and 31 December 2025, a foreign tourist who paid for cosmetic medical
services in South Korea could reclaim the 10% value-added tax included in the price. The mechanism was
not a general tourist tax refund. It was a specific measure written into Article 107-3 of the
Restriction of Special Taxation Act — a statute that exists to grant time-limited tax exceptions,
each with an expiry date attached.</p>
<p>That expiry date is the whole story. The article limits the benefit to qualifying services
supplied "up to 31 December 2025". The provision had been extended repeatedly since it was
introduced; the 2025 tax reform did not extend it again. As displayed on the National Law
Information Center on 8 September 2026, the statute was last amended on 23 December 2025 and took
effect on 1 January 2026 with that end date intact.</p>
<p>The practical result is simple. A cosmetic procedure supplied in South Korea from 1 January 2026 carries
10% VAT, and there is nothing to reclaim on the way home.</p>
<h2 id="who-this-affected">Who this affected</h2>
<p>The scheme covered foreign tourists receiving cosmetic medical services — the category that
includes skin treatments, injectables, and aesthetic surgery. It did not apply to Korean nationals
or to residents, and it did not apply to medical treatment that is VAT-exempt in the ordinary
course, such as care for disease.</p>
<p>It also never applied at every clinic. A clinic could only issue a refund if it was registered to
treat foreign patients and had a contract with a refund operator designated by the National Tax
Service, with the operator's equipment installed. In practice that meant a patient had to ask in
advance, and many clinics that advertised "tax refund" were quietly outside the scheme even while
it ran.</p>
<h2 id="why-you-will-still-see-the-refund-advertised">Why you will still see the refund advertised</h2>
<p>This is the part worth being careful about. The Korea Tourism Organization's own English page on
the subject, dated 20 December 2023, still states that "a system for refunding value-added tax
(VAT) on cosmetic surgery procedures for foreign tourists has been in effect since April 1, 2016."
It has not been revised. Clinic pages, agency blogs, and English-language guides written before
2026 — or copied from those official pages — repeat it.</p>
<p>None of that changes the statute. When an official page and the law disagree, the law governs, and
the law here is unusually easy to read: an article with an end date, and no amendment extending it.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-check-this-yourself">How to check this yourself</h2>
<p>The primary document is Article 107-3 of the Restriction of Special Taxation Act on the National
Law Information Center, linked in the sources below. Two things to look for on that page:</p>
<ul>
<li>the qualifying period stated in the article — whether it still reads 31 December 2025, or has
been amended to a later date by a subsequent tax reform; and</li>
<li>the amendment history at the top, which gives the act number and the date the current version
took effect.</li>
</ul>
<p>If a later amendment revives the benefit, that page will show it before any tourism or clinic site
does. Korean tax provisions of this kind have been extended six times before, so a future
reinstatement is not implausible — which is exactly why the primary source, and not a guide, is
what you should check before you travel.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-do-about-a-quote-that-still-includes-it">What to do about a quote that still includes it</h2>
<p>If a clinic or an agency is quoting you a price "after tax refund" for a procedure in 2026, that
quote is wrong, and the difference is 10% of the bill. Ask which refund operator they are
contracted with and under what provision the refund would be paid. A clinic that cannot answer
that has either not updated its pricing or is describing a discount it intends to fund itself —
and those are very different things to be told before you commit.</p>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>The 10% VAT refund for foreign patients having cosmetic procedures in South Korea applied to services
supplied on or before 31 December 2025 and was not extended. Procedures from 1 January 2026 carry
the full VAT with no refund available. Budget from the quoted price, ignore any "tax refund"
figure in an estimate, and check Article 107-3 directly if you want to know whether that has
