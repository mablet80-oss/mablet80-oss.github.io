Approved in Korea, approved at home?

Many products used in Seoul clinics are Korean brands that are little known abroad. This table shows, for 55 toxins, fillers, boosters, threads and devices, what the Korean, US, Australian and Japanese registers say, with a link to the record. It states registration only. It says nothing about how well a product works or whether it suits you.

Checked 2026-11-04. Full records, with every number and note: approval-status-2026-11.csv.

How to read the table

Approved.
The register shows an approval: an MFDS permit in Korea, an FDA approval in the US, a 承認 in Japan.
Cleared.
The usual US route for a device: an FDA 510(k) or De Novo clearance.
On register.
Included in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG).
Certified.
A Japanese 認証, the route for some lower-risk devices.
Not found.
The register was searched under the product’s names and maker and showed nothing. A product can be sold abroad under another name, so this is not the same as banned.
Not checked.
The register could not be searched on the check date. On 2026-11-04 the Australian register’s search was unreachable, so the Australian column shows only the 18 records that could be opened directly.

An approval covers the use written on the record. Several Korean radiofrequency devices, for example, are registered in Korea and the US as general electrosurgical devices for coagulating tissue rather than for lifting. The summary under each product says so where it applies.

How this was checked

Each cell comes from the register itself: 의약품안전나라 and the 의료기기 database for Korea, Drugs@FDA and the FDA device databases for the US, ARTG public summaries for Australia, and PMDA records for Japan. A maker’s statement that a product is approved somewhere was not counted. Where one name covers several models, the record and the model it names are in the CSV.
