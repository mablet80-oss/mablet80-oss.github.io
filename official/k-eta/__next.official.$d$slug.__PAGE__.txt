21:T11c6,<h2 id="what-k-eta-is-and-what-it-is-not">What K-ETA is, and what it is not</h2>
<p>K-ETA is an electronic travel authorisation. You apply online before you fly, and an approval means
an airline will board you and a border officer will assess you on arrival.</p>
<p>It exists only for people whose nationality already allows entry to South Korea without a visa. It
is not a visa, it does not replace one, and it does not grant entry by itself.</p>
<p>That distinction settles most of the confusion around it. If your passport needs a visa for South
Korea, you are on the visa track and K-ETA is not part of your journey.</p>
<h2 id="the-exemption-and-the-date-on-it">The exemption, and the date on it</h2>
<p>South Korea has suspended the K-ETA requirement for a list of countries and regions, and the
suspension has been extended more than once. The current end date is 31 December 2026.</p>
<p>For this site's readers the practical shape is:</p>
<p><strong>Exempt.</strong> Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao and Singapore. Australia and New Zealand. Also Canada,
the United States and Guam, and fifteen European countries.</p>
<p><strong>Not exempt, but visa-free.</strong> Thailand and Malaysia. This is the pairing that catches people out,
because both nationalities enter South Korea without a visa and both are routinely described
alongside Taiwan and Singapore in travel coverage. Neither is on the exemption list, so K-ETA
approval is still required before departure.</p>
<p><strong>Not applicable.</strong> China, Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines and Mongolia, among others. These
nationalities need a visa, and the visa is the thing to arrange.</p>
<p>Published counts of the exempt list vary between 22 and 25 depending on how territories are
grouped. The official K-ETA site is the version that decides, and it is worth opening it yourself
rather than trusting any count, including this one.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-costs-and-how-long-it-takes">What it costs and how long it takes</h2>
<p>The fee is 10,000 Korean won, roughly seven to eight US dollars.</p>
<p>Processing is generally completed within 72 hours, and the guidance is to apply at least 72 hours
before departure. The only real failure mode here is leaving it until the day, which converts a
ten-minute form into a missed flight.</p>
<p>An approval issued before the exemption began stays valid until its own expiry date, so an existing
K-ETA is not wasted.</p>
<h2 id="why-an-exempt-traveller-might-apply-anyway">Why an exempt traveller might apply anyway</h2>
<p>There is one benefit and it is small: an approved K-ETA exempts you from filling in a paper arrival
card when you land.</p>
<p>The fee applies either way and is not refundable. So it is a convenience purchase, and most exempt
travellers reasonably skip it.</p>
<h2 id="how-this-connects-to-treatment-travel">How this connects to treatment travel</h2>
<p>K-ETA turns on your nationality, not your reason for travelling, so a trip for a skin treatment is
assessed exactly like any other visit.</p>
<p>What a treatment trip can change is whether visa-free entry is the right route in the first place.
South Korea has dedicated categories for medical travel, the C-3-3 short-term medical tourism visa
and the G-1-10 treatment and recuperation visa, and those matter most for longer stays, for
accompanying carers, and for nationalities that need a visa regardless. Our page on
<a href="/official/medical-visa/">the medical visa categories</a> covers when each applies.</p>
<p>For a short outpatient course on a visa-free entry, no special status is involved.</p>
<h2 id="checking-this-for-yourself">Checking this for yourself</h2>
<p>Entry rules change on political timetables, and this exemption has already been extended several
times.</p>
<p>Two habits keep you current. Open the official K-ETA site and read the exemption notice with its
date rather than a summary of it. Then check your own nationality against the list rather than
against a region, because as Thailand and Malaysia show, neighbouring countries do not always sit
on the same side of it.</p>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>Most travellers from Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao, Singapore, Australia and New Zealand need
nothing before boarding for the rest of 2026.</p>
<p>Travellers from Thailand and Malaysia do need K-ETA, despite entering visa-free. Everyone else in
the region is on the visa track instead. Ten minutes and 10,000 won settle it, as long as it
