K-ETA in 2026: who is exempt, and who still needs one

The temporary exemption runs to 31 December 2026 and covers 25 countries and regions. Thailand and Malaysia are visa-free but not on it.

Short answer

Do I need K-ETA to travel to South Korea in 2026?

For most of the region, no. A temporary exemption running to 31 December 2026 covers 25 countries and regions, including Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao, Singapore, Australia and New Zealand, so travellers from those places can board without applying. Thailand and Malaysia are the ones people get wrong: both enter South Korea visa-free, neither is on the exemption list, so nationals of both still need K-ETA approval before departure. If your nationality needs a visa in the first place, K-ETA is not part of your route at all.

What K-ETA is, and what it is not

K-ETA is an electronic travel authorisation. You apply online before you fly, and an approval means an airline will board you and a border officer will assess you on arrival.

It exists only for people whose nationality already allows entry to South Korea without a visa. It is not a visa, it does not replace one, and it does not grant entry by itself.

That distinction settles most of the confusion around it. If your passport needs a visa for South Korea, you are on the visa track and K-ETA is not part of your journey.

The exemption, and the date on it

South Korea has suspended the K-ETA requirement for a list of countries and regions, and the suspension has been extended more than once. The current end date is 31 December 2026.

For this site's readers the practical shape is:

Exempt. Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao and Singapore. Australia and New Zealand. Also Canada, the United States and Guam, and fifteen European countries.

Not exempt, but visa-free. Thailand and Malaysia. This is the pairing that catches people out, because both nationalities enter South Korea without a visa and both are routinely described alongside Taiwan and Singapore in travel coverage. Neither is on the exemption list, so K-ETA approval is still required before departure.

Not applicable. China, Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines and Mongolia, among others. These nationalities need a visa, and the visa is the thing to arrange.

Published counts of the exempt list vary between 22 and 25 depending on how territories are grouped. The official K-ETA site is the version that decides, and it is worth opening it yourself rather than trusting any count, including this one.

What it costs and how long it takes

The fee is 10,000 Korean won, roughly seven to eight US dollars.

Processing is generally completed within 72 hours, and the guidance is to apply at least 72 hours before departure. The only real failure mode here is leaving it until the day, which converts a ten-minute form into a missed flight.

An approval issued before the exemption began stays valid until its own expiry date, so an existing K-ETA is not wasted.

Why an exempt traveller might apply anyway

There is one benefit and it is small: an approved K-ETA exempts you from filling in a paper arrival card when you land.

The fee applies either way and is not refundable. So it is a convenience purchase, and most exempt travellers reasonably skip it.

How this connects to treatment travel

K-ETA turns on your nationality, not your reason for travelling, so a trip for a skin treatment is assessed exactly like any other visit.

What a treatment trip can change is whether visa-free entry is the right route in the first place. South Korea has dedicated categories for medical travel, the C-3-3 short-term medical tourism visa and the G-1-10 treatment and recuperation visa, and those matter most for longer stays, for accompanying carers, and for nationalities that need a visa regardless. Our page on the medical visa categories covers when each applies.

For a short outpatient course on a visa-free entry, no special status is involved.

Checking this for yourself

Entry rules change on political timetables, and this exemption has already been extended several times.

Two habits keep you current. Open the official K-ETA site and read the exemption notice with its date rather than a summary of it. Then check your own nationality against the list rather than against a region, because as Thailand and Malaysia show, neighbouring countries do not always sit on the same side of it.

The bottom line

Most travellers from Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao, Singapore, Australia and New Zealand need nothing before boarding for the rest of 2026.

Travellers from Thailand and Malaysia do need K-ETA, despite entering visa-free. Everyone else in the region is on the visa track instead. Ten minutes and 10,000 won settle it, as long as it happens three days before the flight rather than three hours.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need K-ETA if I am coming for a skin treatment?
K-ETA depends on your nationality and not on why you are travelling, so a treatment trip does not change the answer above. What can change is whether a visa-free entry is the right route for you at all. Short outpatient treatment on a visa-free entry is ordinary, but South Korea also has dedicated medical visa categories, and our page on the C-3-3 and G-1-10 categories covers when those apply.
Which countries are exempt?
Twenty-five countries and regions across four groups. In Asia: Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore and Macao. In Oceania: Australia and New Zealand. In the Americas: Canada, the United States and Guam. In Europe: the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Sweden, Finland, Norway, Belgium, Denmark, Austria, Switzerland and Liechtenstein. Published counts differ depending on how regions are grouped, so treat the official K-ETA site as the list that decides.
Is K-ETA the same as a visa?
No. K-ETA is authorisation to board and to be assessed at the border, for people whose nationality already allows visa-free entry. If your nationality requires a visa for South Korea, which is the case for travellers from China, Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines and Mongolia among others, you apply for a visa and K-ETA does not enter into it.
Should I apply even though I am exempt?
Some people do, because an approved K-ETA means you do not fill in a paper arrival card when you land. That is the whole of the benefit. The fee is charged either way and is not refundable, so it is a convenience purchase rather than a requirement.
How long before my flight should I apply?
At least 72 hours before departure. Processing is generally completed within 72 hours, so leaving it to the airport is the one way to turn a simple form into a missed flight.
