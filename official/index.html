Official Resources

The Korean government's own rules, registers and channels, relayed with a link to every document so you can check each one yourself.

  • What Korean law says about brokers, referral fees and clinic ads

    South Korea's Medical Service Act contains a general prohibition: no one may introduce, refer or solicit a patient to a medical institution or medical personnel for profit. The Act provides limited exceptions, and attracting foreign patients is separately regulated by its own statute, which is why registered agencies exist at all. The practical consequence for a patient is that an unregistered intermediary taking a cut of your treatment fee is not operating in a grey area.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • How to check whether a device or injectable is approved in South Korea

    The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety approves medical devices and drugs for use in South Korea and publishes what it has approved. Devices are searchable through the ministry's device information portal and drugs through its drug safety portal. Approval tells you a product is licensed and for what — it does not tell you the treatment is right for you, or that the clinic is using the product the way it was approved for.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • What a Korean clinic must post about its prices — and cannot exceed

    Yes. Aesthetic treatment is non-covered care in South Korea, and the Medical Service Act requires a medical institution to notify its non-covered charges so that patients can easily understand them — and forbids it from collecting more than the amount it has notified or posted. Institutions must also report those prices to the Ministry of Health and Welfare, which surveys and publishes them through the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • Is your Korean clinic registered to treat foreign patients?

    A clinic in South Korea may not treat foreign patients as a business unless it is registered as a foreign patient attraction institution, and agencies that recruit patients must register separately. Registration is administered by the Korea Health Industry Development Institute for the Ministry of Health and Welfare, and registered institutions are required to carry medical malpractice liability insurance. The register is published, and Medical Korea's own advice to patients is to ask the clinic to show its registration certificate.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • KAHF accreditation: the tier above registration in South Korea

    KAHF is South Korea's accreditation programme for institutions serving foreign patients, and it sits above the basic registration every such clinic must hold. An institution must already carry KOIHA healthcare accreditation before it can be assessed, and it is then measured against 44 criteria on foreign-patient management and 92 criteria on patient safety. The accredited institutions are published as a list, and it is short.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • If a Korean clinic goes wrong: the official dispute route

    South Korea has a statutory body, the Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency, that handles medical disputes outside the courts. The government's own plain-language guidance states that an application may be made by a party to the dispute or their representative and sets no nationality condition. The outcomes carry real legal weight: an arbitration decision has the same effect as a final court judgment.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • South Korea's medical visas: C-3-3 and G-1-10

    South Korea has two categories for patients: C-3-3 for short-term treatment, allowing a stay of up to 90 days, and G-1-10 for treatment running beyond 91 days. A caregiver may travel on the same basis as the patient. Only a medical institution or facilitator registered to attract foreign patients can issue the invitation that supports a C-3-3 application, which makes the visa route and the registration scheme the same check.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • South Korea's VAT refund on cosmetic procedures ended on 1 January 2026

    No. The refund was a special measure under Article 107-3 of South Korea's Restriction of Special Taxation Act, and that article limited it to cosmetic medical services supplied on or before 31 December 2025. The 2025 tax reform did not extend it, so procedures from 1 January 2026 carry the full 10% VAT with no refund. Several official and clinic pages have not been updated and still describe the refund as available.

    Updated 2026-09-08
