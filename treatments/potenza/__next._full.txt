1e:T19db,<h2 id="what-microneedle-radiofrequency-is">What microneedle radiofrequency is</h2>
<p>Ordinary radiofrequency devices put energy in through the skin surface, which means the epidermis
takes heat on the way to wherever the treatment is aimed. Microneedle radiofrequency avoids that:
<strong>fine needles carry the current through the epidermis and release it at a depth the operator sets.</strong></p>
<p>So there are two mechanisms working at once — the mechanical injury of the needle and the thermal
injury of the energy at its tip — and one important property: the surface is comparatively spared.</p>
<p>Potenza is one such device. Sylfirm is another. Korean clinics sell them by name rather than by
category, which is why the <a href="/treatments/thermage-flx/">three arrangements</a> are worth knowing.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-evidence-supports">What the evidence supports</h2>

<p>Those ranges are wide, and the width is the honest part: different scar types, devices, needle

"rigorous reporting of device settings" precisely because that variation is not yet controlled for.</p>
<h2 id="the-property-that-makes-it-a-good-fit-here">The property that makes it a good fit here</h2>
<p>For an international audience — and this site's readers are largely in East and Southeast Asia and
Australia — the finding that matters most is about <strong>skin type</strong>.</p>
<p>The review reports <strong>consistent safety across Fitzpatrick skin types</strong>, with post-inflammatory
hyperpigmentation in <strong>2.9–5%</strong> of cases, mild and transient adverse events, and no serious
complications across the included trials. That last finding is about the trials, and the next
section covers what has been reported outside them.</p>
<p>That is a meaningful contrast with ablative and high-fluence laser treatment, where the central risk
in pigmented skin is provoking more pigment. Because the energy is delivered past the epidermis
rather than through it, microneedle radiofrequency sidesteps much of that. It is a large part of why
it is chosen for Asian skin, and it is a better reason than the brand marketing usually given.</p>
<h2 id="what-has-been-reported-outside-the-trials">What has been reported outside the trials</h2>
<p>Trials run under set protocols, with selected patients and trained operators. Reports from ordinary
use are a different kind of evidence, and they belong beside the review rather than under it.</p>
<p>On <strong>15 October 2025 the US FDA issued a safety communication on RF microneedling</strong>. It describes
reports of <strong>burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement and nerve damage</strong>, including cases that needed
surgical repair or other medical treatment.</p>
<p>Two limits keep that in proportion. The notice <strong>names no brand</strong>, so it is not a finding against
Potenza in particular. And reports of harm show that it happens, not how often, which is the figure
the review's 2.9–5% speaks to inside its trials.</p>
<p>The FDA's advice for patients works as a checklist in Korea too: a licensed provider trained on the
device, the device named before treatment, a conversation about the alternatives, and medical care
promptly if burning, scarring or a change in contour follows.</p>
<h2 id="acne-scars-specifically">Acne scars specifically</h2>
<p>This is where the evidence is strongest and where the treatment is most worth considering. Scar
scores fell by 26–100% across the studies, and the reviewers describe efficacy as comparable to
fractional lasers.</p>
<p>What that does not mean is a single-session fix. Atrophic scars respond over a course and continue
remodelling for months afterwards, and the type of scar matters — different scar shapes respond at
different rates. A clinic that assesses your scar types before proposing a number of sessions is
doing the right thing; one that quotes a package before looking is not.</p>
<h2 id="brand-against-brand">Brand against brand</h2>
<p>Potenza and Sylfirm are both sold with claims of distinct advantages. The systematic review's closing
request — standardised outcomes, longer follow-up, and rigorous reporting of device settings to
"facilitate head-to-head device comparisons" — tells you those comparisons have not been done.</p>
<p>So a claim that one device outperforms another is, at present, unevidenced. What is evidenced is the
category.</p>
<h2 id="questions-to-ask-a-korean-clinic">Questions to ask a Korean clinic</h2>
<ul>
<li>Which device, and what is its Korean product name?</li>
<li>What needle depth and energy level, and why those for my skin?</li>
<li>Are the needles insulated, and what difference does that make here?</li>
<li>What scar types do I have, and how does that change the plan?</li>
<li>How many sessions, at what interval, and what is the total?</li>
<li>Who will operate the device, and what training do they have on it?</li>
</ul>
