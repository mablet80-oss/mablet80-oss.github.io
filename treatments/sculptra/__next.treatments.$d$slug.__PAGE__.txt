21:T134e,<h2 id="what-it-is-and-what-it-is-not">What it is, and what it is not</h2>
<p><strong>Poly-L-lactic acid</strong> microparticles, reconstituted with sterile water into a suspension and
injected. Over the following months the particles are broken down and the tissue lays down collagen
in response.</p>
<p>So it is a <strong>collagen stimulator, not a filler</strong>. Nothing is being placed to occupy a space. On the
day of treatment you may look slightly fuller from the injected fluid, and that is not the result —
it goes, and the real change arrives over three to six months.</p>
<p>Two consequences follow, and they are the reason to read the rest of this page:</p>
<ul>
<li>You cannot judge it early.</li>
<li>You cannot undo it.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-the-evidence-shows">What the evidence shows</h2>
<p>A <strong>2024 systematic review</strong> in <em>Polymers</em> pooled <strong>11 randomised trials</strong> covering roughly <strong>1,169
participants</strong>.</p>
<p>Four studies found increased <strong>dermal thickness</strong> and significant improvement in facial lipoatrophy,
with effects <strong>sustained for at least 25 months</strong>. Two found PLLA superior to injectable human
collagen; three found it better than polyacrylamide hydrogel on lipoatrophy severity, skin quality,
elasticity and satisfaction.</p>
<p>Then the reviewers' own verdict, which is the sentence to carry: PLLA is <em>"a highly effective and
long-lasting treatment for facial aesthetics with a reasonable safety profile. However, all findings
are supported by low-quality evidence."</em> Five of the eleven studies carried a high risk of bias.</p>
<p>Long-lasting and well liked. Not well proven.</p>
<h2 id="the-nodule-question-and-why-the-numbers-disagree">The nodule question, and why the numbers disagree</h2>
<p>Nodules are the complication that defines this treatment, and the published rates are wildly
inconsistent — which is itself the finding.</p>
<ul>
<li>The 2024 systematic review reports nodule rates across studies of <strong>6% to 41%</strong>.</li>
<li>A <strong>2026 scoping review of 40 studies</strong> puts incidence at <strong>1.2% to 14%</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p>The second review explains the gap, and the explanation is useful: <strong>dilution volume</strong>. Early
practice reconstituted a vial in <strong>2–3 mL</strong>, and those studies had much higher nodule rates. At
<strong>8–10 mL or more per vial</strong> — now typical for facial treatment — the rate falls to roughly <strong>1.3–4%</strong>.</p>
<p>Two more details worth having before a consultation:</p>
<p><strong>Timing.</strong> Nodules appear in two patterns — <strong>early at 4–8 weeks</strong> from particle aggregation, or
<strong>delayed beyond six months</strong>, most commonly at <strong>9–14 months</strong> and sometimes past two years. A good
result at three months does not mean you are clear.</p>
<p><strong>Site.</strong> The scoping review names the <strong>periorbital region, temples and nasolabial folds</strong> as
higher risk, requiring careful technique.</p>
<h2 id="nothing-dissolves-it">Nothing dissolves it</h2>
<p>Hyaluronidase breaks down hyaluronic acid. It does nothing to poly-L-lactic acid.</p>
<p>That is why the scoping review's conclusion is about prevention rather than treatment: <em>"Prevention
through adequate dilution and correct injection technique remains the most evidence-supported
approach."</em></p>
<p>For a patient, this converts into a simple rule. <strong>Start conservatively.</strong> A course can always be
extended; 25 months of a result you dislike cannot be shortened.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-ask-at-a-consultation">What to ask at a consultation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>How many millilitres are you reconstituting each vial with?</strong> This is the single question most
connected to the published nodule rates, and a clinic that does this often will answer it without
hesitation.</li>
<li><strong>How many sessions, at what interval, and what is the total cost?</strong> It is a course.</li>
<li><strong>When should I judge the result?</strong> The answer should be months, not weeks.</li>
<li><strong>What is the plan if a nodule appears at nine months?</strong> Ask before, not after.</li>
<li><strong>Which product, in Korean?</strong> Several PLLA products are sold in South Korea beyond the original
brand. Check the licence.</li>
</ul>

dilution volume the variable that separates them, and with onset sometimes past a year. Nothing
