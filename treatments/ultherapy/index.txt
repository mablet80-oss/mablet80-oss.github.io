1e:T15c0,<h2 id="what-ultherapy-is">What Ultherapy is</h2>
<p>Ultherapy delivers <strong>microfocused ultrasound</strong> — ultrasound energy focused to a point below the skin
surface, hot enough at that point to create a small zone of thermal injury, which the body then
repairs. The repair is the treatment: new collagen laid down over the following months, and tissue
that behaves slightly tighter as a result.</p>
<p>The "with visualization" part matters and is what the V in MFU-V stands for. The device images the
tissue before it fires, so the operator can see the layers they are treating rather than working
blind.</p>
<h2 id="why-the-depth-is-the-whole-argument">Why the depth is the whole argument</h2>
<p>Focused ultrasound is delivered at fixed depths. The shallow settings work in the dermis. The
deepest — around 4.5 mm — reaches the <strong>SMAS</strong>, the fibrous layer a surgeon pulls on during a
facelift.</p>
<p>That is the entire case for the technology, and it is also the case's limit. Reaching the SMAS with
heat is not the same as pulling on it with sutures. The effect is a tightening of tissue that is
already where it should be, not a relocation of tissue that has fallen.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-evidence-shows">What the evidence shows</h2>
<p>A <strong>2024 systematic review and meta-analysis in the Aesthetic Surgery Journal</strong> screened 4,019
references and pooled 42 studies. Its findings, with the numbers as published:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Investigator-assessed improvement: 89%</strong> (95% CI 81–94%)</li>
<li><strong>Patient-reported improvement: 84%</strong> (95% CI 73–91%)</li>
<li><strong>Satisfaction: 84% overall</strong>, 93% for the face and neck</li>
<li><strong>Mean pain: 4.85 out of 10</strong></li>
<li>Adverse events — redness, swelling, oedema, bruising, tenderness — mild to moderate, with <strong>no
serious adverse events or long-term sequelae reported</strong></li>
</ul>
<p>One figure in that list deserves separating out, because it says something about how satisfaction
data in aesthetics is produced. Pooled satisfaction was 84% — but <strong>62% in the studies that gave
patients a neutral option</strong> rather than making them choose satisfied or dissatisfied. The same
treatment scores twenty points differently depending on how the question is asked. When a clinic
quotes you a satisfaction percentage, that is the question to ask about it.</p>
<p>The authors' own summary is measured: an enhancement in aesthetic score and satisfaction, a moderate
pain score, and a generally favourable safety profile. Not a facelift, and not nothing.</p>
<h2 id="shurink-ultraformer-and-the-korean-devices">Shurink, Ultraformer, and the Korean devices</h2>
<p>A visitor to a Korean clinic will more often be offered <strong>Shurink (슈링크)</strong>, <strong>Ultraformer
(울트라포머)</strong>, <strong>Doublo (더블로)</strong> or <strong>Liftera</strong> than Ultherapy itself. These are focused-ultrasound
devices in the same category, usually at a lower price per session.</p>
<p>Two honest points about that. First, the evidence above is for microfocused ultrasound as a class —
it does not establish that any one of these performs like any other, and a clinic asserting a
specific brand advantage should be able to say on what basis. Second, the difference you can
actually verify is regulatory: ask for the device's Korean product name and look up its licence.</p>
<h2 id="side-effects-and-who-does-it">Side effects and who does it</h2>
<p>Reported effects are redness, swelling, and tenderness, mild to moderate and short-lived. The
meta-analysis reported no serious adverse events across its 42 studies.</p>
<p>The caveat is operator-dependent. Focused ultrasound is delivered at chosen depths and energy
levels, in chosen positions, and getting those wrong is how the uncommon problems — fat loss where
it was not wanted, nerve irritation — happen. Who holds the handpiece matters more than which logo
is on the machine.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-does-not-do">What it does not do</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>It does not reposition tissue.</strong> Descended tissue is a thread lift or surgery question.</li>
<li><strong>It does not add volume.</strong> A hollow is a filler question.</li>
<li><strong>It does not resurface skin.</strong> Texture and pigment are laser questions.</li>
<li><strong>It does not work on the day.</strong> The change develops over months, which is also why judging it
too early is a common disappointment.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="questions-to-ask-a-korean-clinic">Questions to ask a Korean clinic</h2>
<ul>
<li>Which device, and what is its Korean product name? Look up its licence.</li>
<li>How many shots, at which depths, and over which areas?</li>
<li>Who operates it — and are they a doctor?</li>
<li>What are you expecting this to change, specifically, and when should I judge it?</li>
<li>If you are also proposing a thread lift or filler, what is each one for?</li>
</ul>
