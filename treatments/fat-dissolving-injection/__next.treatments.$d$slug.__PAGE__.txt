1d:T172a,<h2 id="two-different-things-share-the-name">Two different things share the name</h2>
<p>This is the whole page in one distinction, and almost no clinic menu makes it.</p>
<p><strong>A licensed drug.</strong> Deoxycholic acid, approved by the ministry for fat under the chin, with a fixed
formulation, a fixed dose, and randomised trials behind it.</p>
<p><strong>A compounded mixture.</strong> Prepared in the clinic, sold as <strong>윤곽주사</strong>, containing whatever the
clinic has chosen to put in it. No licence, no fixed formula, and no established dose.</p>
<p>Both are injections. Both are described as dissolving fat. They are not the same product, and the
evidence for one is not evidence for the other.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-licensed-in-south-korea">What is licensed in South Korea</h2>
<p><strong>Two products</strong>, both deoxycholic acid, both for submental fat: <strong>브이올렛</strong>, approved in 2021, and
<strong>벨라콜린</strong>, approved in 2024.</p>
<p><strong>Kybella</strong> — 벨카이라, the first such product approved anywhere — <strong>left the South Korean market in
2020</strong>. If a clinic offers you Kybella here, ask what they are actually injecting.</p>
<p>Anything else offered for fat anywhere else on the face or body is off-label at best.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-evidence-says-for-the-licensed-version">What the evidence says for the licensed version</h2>
<p>A <strong>2023 meta-analysis</strong> pooled <strong>five randomised trials in 1,837 patients</strong>. Against placebo, at the
2 mg/cm² dose:</p>
<ul>
<li>Clinician-rated improvement of at least one point — <strong>RR 2.28</strong> (95% CI 2.04–2.54)</li>
<li>Clinician-rated improvement of at least two points — <strong>RR 5.30</strong> (3.85–7.30)</li>
<li>Patient-rated improvement of at least one point — <strong>RR 2.02</strong> (1.83–2.22)</li>
<li>Volume reduction of 10% or more — <strong>RR 8.43</strong> (5.73–12.43)</li>
</ul>
<p>That is a real effect on a small, defined problem.</p>
<p>Two things to hold alongside it. The side effects are <strong>common</strong>, if minor: numbness was <strong>13 times</strong>
more likely than on placebo, fibrosis about <strong>10 times</strong>, nodules about <strong>6 times</strong>. And <strong>all five
trials were funded by the manufacturers</strong> — the reviewers say so themselves, rate the certainty as
<strong>low to moderate</strong>, and call for trials that are not industry-sponsored.</p>
<h2 id="what-south-koreas-own-agency-found-about-the-rest">What South Korea's own agency found about the rest</h2>
<p>This is the part worth travelling with.</p>
<p><strong>NECA</strong> — 한국보건의료연구원, the national health technology assessment agency — reassessed
fat-dissolving injections in <strong>June 2024</strong>, reviewing <strong>38 studies</strong>.</p>
<p>Its findings, in its own framing:</p>
<ul>
<li>Most studies <strong>could not confirm a long-term effect</strong>, with <strong>no meaningful difference</strong> against
placebo or no treatment.</li>
<li>For the compounded injections, the <strong>drug types, mixing ratios, doses and injection schedules have
never been clearly established</strong>. The agents in use include deoxycholic acid, choline
alfoscerate, aminophylline, <strong>steroids</strong>, caffeine, hyaluronidase, carnitine and verapamil.</li>
<li>Rare but serious events are documented: <strong>bacterial infection, skin necrosis, foreign-body
granuloma, drug urticaria</strong>, and <strong>acute toxic reactions requiring intensive care</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p>Read the second point carefully. It is not that the mixture is badly chosen. It is that <strong>there is no
standard to choose against</strong> — so what you receive depends on the clinic, and no two are obliged to
match.</p>
<h2 id="what-this-changes-for-a-visitor">What this changes for a visitor</h2>
<p>You are unlikely to be handed a product name unprompted, and "지방분해주사" on a price list tells you
nothing about which of the two categories you are in.</p>
<p>So ask, in this order:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Is this a licensed drug or a mixture prepared here?</strong></li>
<li>If licensed — <strong>which product</strong>, and is it 브이올렛 or 벨라콜린?</li>
<li>If a mixture — <strong>what is in it, and in what proportion?</strong> NECA's finding is that no standard
exists, so the answer is specific to this clinic.</li>
<li><strong>What area is being treated?</strong> The licensed evidence is for under the chin. Elsewhere is
off-label.</li>
<li>Look the product name up on the <strong>ministry's drug portal</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="what-it-will-not-do">What it will not do</h2>
<p>Neither version is a weight-loss treatment, and neither substitutes for surgery on a larger volume
of fat. The licensed evidence is for a <strong>small, defined pocket under the chin</strong>, measured in
one-point improvements on a five-point scale. A clinic proposing it for a body contour is proposing
something the trials did not test.</p>
<p>If the concern is a soft jawline rather than a fat pad, that is usually a tightening or
repositioning question, and belongs to a different page.</p>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>Two products are licensed in South Korea for submental fat, both deoxycholic acid, and Kybella is
not one of them — it left the market in 2020. The licensed version has genuine randomised evidence
with frequent minor side effects and an acknowledged industry-funding problem across every trial.
The 윤곽주사 sold far more widely is a compounded mixture with no established formula, which South
Korea's own health technology agency reviewed across 38 studies without confirming a long-term
effect, and for which it documents rare cases of necrosis, granuloma and toxic reactions needing
intensive care.
