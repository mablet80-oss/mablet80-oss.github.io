1c:T12a9,<h2 id="what-laser-toning-is">What laser toning is</h2>
<p><strong>Repeated low-energy passes</strong> of a 1064 nm Q-switched Nd:YAG laser across an area, rather than
targeted shots at individual marks. The idea is to break down pigment gradually, below the threshold
that would injure the surrounding skin.</p>
<p>It is a <strong>technique, not a device</strong>. The same word is sold on several laser platforms, and in South
Korea you will see it as 레이저토닝, 듀얼토닝, 피코토닝 and 제네시스토닝 depending on the machine. So
"toning" tells you what is being done, not what with — ask which laser and at what setting.</p>
<p>The published protocol is fairly consistent: around <strong>10 sessions</strong>, weekly or biweekly, at
<strong>0.5–3.8 J/cm²</strong> with a 6–10 mm spot.</p>
<h2 id="it-beat-the-expensive-option">It beat the expensive option</h2>
<p>This is the finding worth travelling with, because it runs against how these treatments are priced.</p>
<p>A <strong>2026 meta-analysis of 52 randomised trials</strong> covering 1,058 patients tested laser therapies for
melasma. <strong>Low-fluence Q-switched Nd:YAG</strong> — the laser behind classic toning — showed a
<strong>significant improvement over controls</strong> (MD 1.47; 95% CI 0.08 to 2.85). <strong>Picosecond lasers</strong>, the
newer and more costly technology, <strong>did not</strong> (MD −0.11; 95% CI −0.79 to 0.57).</p>
<p>The older technique is the one with the result. A clinic steering you toward a pico platform for
melasma on the grounds that it is newer is not citing this.</p>
<h2 id="the-complication-nobody-quotes">The complication nobody quotes</h2>
<p>Now the part that should shape your decision, and it is specific to readers with East Asian skin.</p>
<p>A <strong>2022 systematic review</strong> of 42 studies and <strong>1,736 melasma patients</strong> reports <strong>mottled
hypopigmentation</strong> in around <strong>10% of East Asian patients</strong> — small pale spots, described in the
literature as punctate leukoderma or a confetti pattern, where too much pigment has been removed.</p>
<p>Three details make it serious:</p>
<ul>
<li>It lasts <strong>two to three years</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Fewer than 10%</strong> resolve on their own.</li>
<li>It is associated with <strong>excessive cumulative laser energy</strong> — the total across the course, not the
strength of one session.</li>
</ul>
<p>That last point inverts the usual reassurance. "We use very low energy" is not protective if the
answer to "how many sessions?" is open-ended. The risk is in the running total.</p>
<p>Pigment can also fail in the other direction: <strong>rebound hyperpigmentation</strong> was reported more often
in <strong>Fitzpatrick types IV–V</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="it-comes-back">It comes back</h2>
<p>Melasma is chronic, and the recurrence figures are both high and unstable across studies. At three
months, reported recurrence ranges from <strong>64–81%</strong> in some studies to <strong>100%</strong> in others. At one
year, <strong>58.8% relapse</strong>.</p>
<p>The reviewers' own recommendation follows from that: combine toning with other melasma treatment
rather than relying on it alone, which reduces both recurrence and adverse events.</p>
<p>So the honest framing is <strong>management, not cure</strong> — and a course sold as a fix is being sold against
its own literature.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-ask-at-a-consultation">What to ask at a consultation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Which laser platform, and what fluence?</strong> Get the Korean product name and check it on the MFDS
device portal.</li>
<li><strong>How many sessions in total, and is that capped?</strong> An open-ended plan is the shape the risk takes.</li>
<li><strong>What else are you using alongside it?</strong> The reviewers say combination is what reduces recurrence.</li>
<li><strong>What is the plan if pale spots appear?</strong> Ask before, not after.</li>
<li><strong>What happens when I stop?</strong> For melasma, maintenance is the normal case.</li>
</ul>
<p>A clinic that brings up mottled hypopigmentation before you do has read the same literature you just
did.</p>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>For melasma, laser toning is the technique with the pooled evidence behind it — significantly better
than controls across 52 randomised trials, where picosecond lasers were not. But roughly 10% of East
Asian patients develop mottled hypopigmentation that persists for two to three years and rarely
clears on its own, and it accumulates with the number of sessions. Melasma also recurs in most
people, with 58.8% relapsing within a year. Treat it as a managed condition, cap the course, and
