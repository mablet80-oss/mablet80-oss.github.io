22:T13f9,<h2 id="what-a-peel-is">What a peel is</h2>
<p>A <strong>controlled chemical injury</strong>. An acid removes the outer layers of skin, and the skin that
replaces them is more even in pigment and texture.</p>
<p>The variable that matters is <strong>depth</strong>, not brand. Superficial peels reach the epidermis; deeper
ones go further, work harder, and carry more risk — particularly in skin that pigments easily. In
South Korea you will see 필링 on almost every price list, usually as the cheapest item, and usually
without the depth stated.</p>
<p>Common agents: <strong>glycolic acid</strong>, <strong>salicylic acid</strong>, <strong>Jessner's solution</strong>, <strong>trichloroacetic
acid</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="the-agent-that-won-was-the-mild-one">The agent that won was the mild one</h2>
<p>A <strong>meta-analysis of ten randomised trials and three prospective studies</strong>, covering <strong>478 patients
with darker skin types</strong>, tested peels as single agents for melasma.</p>
<p><strong>Glycolic acid outperformed trichloroacetic acid</strong> on the melasma area and severity index — MD
<strong>−1.89</strong> (95% CI −3.26 to −0.52; p = 0.007).</p>
<p>This is the second time on this site that the gentler, cheaper option has come out ahead of the
aggressive one for melasma; laser toning did the same against picosecond lasers. It is worth
noticing as a pattern rather than a coincidence: <strong>melasma responds poorly to force</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="peels-against-creams">Peels against creams</h2>
<p>The same analysis compared peels with topical treatments, and the results cut both ways.</p>
<p><strong>Where peels won:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Trichloroacetic acid beat topical hydroquinone — MD <strong>−5.30</strong> (p &#x3C; 0.001)</li>
<li>Jessner's solution beat topical hydroquinone — MD <strong>−3.20</strong> (p = 0.004)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Where they did not:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Glycolic acid did <strong>not</strong> beat tretinoin (MD 0.53; p = 0.3)</li>
<li>Nor vitamin C iontophoresis (MD 1.50; p = 0.14)</li>
<li>Nor amino fruit acid (MD 0.39; p = 0.46)</li>
</ul>
<p>So a peel is better than one particular cream, and not better than several others. That is a
narrower claim than a clinic menu implies.</p>
<h2 id="the-part-that-decides-your-result">The part that decides your result</h2>
<p>The reviewers' conclusion contains a qualifier that is easy to read past, and it is the whole
practical lesson: peels are effective for melasma in darker skin <strong>"with the use of topicals as
maintenance treatment"</strong>.</p>
<p>A <strong>2025 retrospective review</strong> puts a number on what happens without it. A sequential glycolic acid
and trichloroacetic acid protocol produced significant improvement at twelve weeks — and melasma
<strong>recurrence reached 70% by week 12 after treatment stopped</strong>.</p>
<p>For someone flying in for a course of peels, that reframes the purchase. The peels are the
beginning. What you use for the following year is the treatment.</p>
<p>Ask what maintenance topical is expected, and whether you can obtain it at home, <strong>before</strong> you book
the course.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-side-effects-look-like">What the side effects look like</h2>
<p>From the same 2025 study — and note the population is <strong>Fitzpatrick IV–VI African patients</strong>, not
East Asian skin, so read the direction rather than the exact numbers:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Irritation</strong> — 77.5%</li>
<li><strong>Post-peel cracking</strong> — 62.5%</li>
<li><strong>Transient hyperpigmentation</strong> — 12.5%</li>
<li><strong>Scarring</strong> — none observed</li>
</ul>
<p>The common complications are uncomfortable rather than dangerous, and the one to plan around is
<strong>temporary darkening</strong>, which is exactly the thing you came to treat. It is a reason to have a
first session well before any event, and to ask what the clinic does if it happens.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-ask-at-a-consultation">What to ask at a consultation</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Which agent, at what concentration, and to what depth?</strong></li>
<li><strong>How many sessions, at what interval, and what is the total?</strong></li>
<li><strong>What topical will I be on afterwards, and for how long?</strong> If there is no answer, the plan is
incomplete.</li>
<li><strong>What do you do if I darken after a session</strong> — and can that be handled before I fly home?</li>
<li>If a device-based peel system is used, <strong>what is its Korean product name</strong>?</li>
</ul>
