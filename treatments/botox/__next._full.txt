23:T13a5,<h2 id="what-botulinum-toxin-does">What botulinum toxin does</h2>
<p>A muscle contracts because a nerve tells it to, using a chemical signal released where the nerve
meets the muscle. Botulinum toxin blocks that release. The muscle does not receive the instruction,
so it does not contract, so the skin it was folding stops being folded.</p>
<p>Everything useful about this treatment follows from that one sentence, including its limits.</p>
<h2 id="movement-lines-not-resting-lines">Movement lines, not resting lines</h2>
<p>Because the mechanism is muscle relaxation, toxin addresses <strong>lines your face makes when it moves</strong> —
frown lines between the brows, crow's feet, forehead lines.</p>
<p>A line that is visible when your face is completely still is a different thing. It has been etched
into the skin over years, and no muscle is holding it at that moment. Toxin can stop such a line
deepening further, which is a real benefit, but softening what is already there belongs to
resurfacing, boosters, or filler depending on the line.</p>
<p>This is the most common mismatch between what a patient wants and what a toxin injection can give,
and it is worth settling at the consultation rather than afterwards.</p>
<h2 id="how-long-it-lasts">How long it lasts</h2>
<p>For glabellar lines — the frown lines — the clinical literature commonly reports sustained benefit
in the region of three to four months, varying with the product, the dose, and the individual.
Treating several upper-face areas at once has been reported to extend the time before a patient
returns to their untreated state.</p>
<p>What is worth being sceptical of is a single confident number offered without a product attached.
Different toxin formulations have different published durations, and a clinic quoting "six months"
for an unspecified product is not quoting a trial.</p>
<h2 id="the-korean-regulatory-point-worth-knowing">The Korean regulatory point worth knowing</h2>
<p>South Korea has an unusually large domestic botulinum toxin industry, and an international patient is
likely to be offered a product they have never heard of. Recognising the brand is not the useful
check. This is:</p>
<p>Botulinum toxin is a <strong>biological product</strong>, and under the Pharmaceutical Affairs Act every
<strong>manufacturing batch</strong> must receive <strong>national lot release approval</strong> from the Ministry of Food and
Drug Safety before it can be sold. The ministry tests and reviews each lot; product approval alone
is not enough to put a vial on a shelf.</p>
<p>That is a stronger control than many countries apply, and it gives a patient two concrete things to
do: ask which product is being used and look it up on the drug portal, and ask to see the vial
before it is drawn up. Neither request is unusual in a clinic doing things properly.</p>
<h2 id="beyond-wrinkles">Beyond wrinkles</h2>
<p>In South Korea, toxin is very commonly used for <strong>jaw slimming</strong>, injected into the masseter muscle. That
is a muscle-bulk indication rather than a wrinkle one: reduced activity over repeated treatments can
reduce the muscle's size, changing the shape of the lower face. It works on a slower timescale than
wrinkle treatment and is a different conversation about dose and interval.</p>
<p>Other uses you may see offered include treatment for excessive sweating and various "spot" toxin
treatments marketed under their own names. Ask what muscle or gland is being targeted and why.</p>
<h2 id="side-effects-and-what-can-go-wrong">Side effects and what can go wrong</h2>
<p>The common effects are minor: small raised bumps at injection points that settle within hours, and
occasional bruising.</p>
<p>The effects worth discussing beforehand come from the toxin acting on a muscle you did not intend to
treat — a heavy or asymmetric brow, a changed smile. These are temporary, because the effect itself
is temporary, but temporary can mean months. The variable that most affects this is who is injecting
and how well they know the anatomy, which is the same conclusion this site keeps reaching about
Korean aesthetic treatment generally.</p>
<h2 id="questions-to-ask-a-korean-clinic">Questions to ask a Korean clinic</h2>
<ul>
<li>Which toxin product, and what is its Korean name? Look it up on the drug portal.</li>
<li>May I see the vial before it is drawn up?</li>
<li>What dose, into which muscles, and why those?</li>
<li>Who is injecting — and are they a doctor?</li>
<li>What do we do if the result is uneven, and is a review visit included?</li>
</ul>
