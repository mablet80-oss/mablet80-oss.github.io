1e:T12af,<h2 id="what-monopolar-radiofrequency-is">What monopolar radiofrequency is</h2>
<p>Radiofrequency treatment does not heat the skin from outside. It passes an electrical current
through tissue, and the tissue's own resistance turns that current into heat from within. Heated
collagen contracts immediately, and the controlled injury prompts the body to lay down new collagen
over the following months.</p>
<p>Thermage is the monopolar version: <strong>one active electrode on the skin, with the current returning
through a grounding pad</strong> elsewhere on the body. That arrangement is what produces its defining
characteristic — energy spread through a large volume of tissue, deep.</p>
<h2 id="the-three-arrangements-and-why-the-difference-matters">The three arrangements, and why the difference matters</h2>
<p>A Korean clinic will offer you radiofrequency under half a dozen device names without ever
mentioning which arrangement each uses. The arrangement is the useful information.</p>
<p><strong>Monopolar</strong> reaches deepest — up to around <strong>20 mm</strong> according to the 2025 review cited here —
with diffuse energy distribution, and is the most painful. <strong>Bipolar</strong> confines the energy between
two electrodes at <strong>1–4 mm</strong>, which is shallower and more controlled. <strong>Microneedle radiofrequency</strong>
puts the energy through needles, so it is delivered past the epidermis to a depth the operator sets.</p>
<p>Mapped onto what you will actually be offered in Seoul: Thermage, Oligio and Volnewmer are
monopolar; InMode is bipolar; Potenza and Sylfirm are microneedle. The question "which arrangement
is this?" tells you more about what a device will do than its brand name does.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-evidence-reports">What the evidence reports</h2>
<p>The 2025 review in <em>Health Science Reports</em> gathers what has been published. Its headline figures
for monopolar devices:</p>
<ul>
<li>A multicentre trial reporting <strong>83.2% periorbital improvement</strong> and <strong>eyebrow lifting greater than
0.5 mm in 61.5%</strong> of 86 patients.</li>
<li>Satisfaction across studies ranging from <strong>50% to 82%</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>73.5%</strong> observing improvement within two months.</li>
</ul>
<p>That satisfaction range is worth pausing on. A spread from half to four-fifths of patients is not a
rounding difference; it reflects genuinely different results across studies, devices, and operators.
Any single satisfaction figure quoted to you is a selection from that range.</p>
<p>The reviewers' own conclusion is the sentence to take away: efficacy <em>"depends critically on the
customization of treatment parameters and the expertise of the practitioner."</em></p>
<h2 id="pain-cooling-and-what-can-go-wrong">Pain, cooling, and what can go wrong</h2>
<p>Depth costs comfort. Monopolar is the most uncomfortable of the three arrangements, precisely
because it is heating a large volume.</p>
<p>It also carries the clearest hazard, which is burning. These devices apply <strong>surface cooling</strong> to
protect the epidermis while the deeper tissue heats, and the review notes the trade-off explicitly:
more cooling protects the surface but works against the depth you were paying for. Getting that
balance right is a setting the operator chooses, which is the practical meaning of "expertise of the
practitioner".</p>
<h2 id="what-it-does-not-do">What it does not do</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>It does not reposition tissue.</strong> Descended tissue is a thread lift or surgical question.</li>
<li><strong>It does not add volume.</strong> A hollow is a filler question.</li>
<li><strong>It is not immediate in its final form.</strong> There is a contraction effect on the day, but the
substantive change accrues over months, and judging it at two weeks is judging it too early.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="questions-to-ask-a-korean-clinic">Questions to ask a Korean clinic</h2>
<ul>
<li>Which device, and is it monopolar, bipolar, or microneedle?</li>
<li>What is its Korean product name, so I can check the licence?</li>
<li>How many shots and at what energy level, and how is that decided for me?</li>
<li>Who operates it — and are they a doctor?</li>
<li>If you are also proposing focused ultrasound, what is each one for?</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>Monopolar radiofrequency heats a large, deep volume of tissue — up to around 20 mm — contracting
collagen immediately and prompting more over months. Published satisfaction ranges from 50% to 82%,
which is wide, and the reviewers attribute the variation to treatment parameters and operator
expertise rather than to the technology alone. It tightens; it does not lift descended tissue or
