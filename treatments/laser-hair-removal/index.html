Laser hair removal in South Korea: sessions, skin type and timing a trip

Short answer

Can I get laser hair removal done on a trip to Korea?

Yes, but one trip is a start, not a full course. Laser hair removal works over several sessions spaced weeks apart, because each session only reaches the hairs that are actively growing at the time. In one trial in Asian skin, a single session reduced hair by 32% at nine months, against 55% after three. In South Korea it is treated as a medical procedure, and Korean clinics commonly offer lasers suited to darker skin types.

What is laser hair removal?

Laser hair removal uses a laser to heat the pigment in hair, damaging the follicle so that regrowth slows. It gives long-term reduction rather than guaranteed permanent removal.

In South Korea it is known as 레이저 제모, or simply 제모, and it is one of the most widely offered treatments in skin clinics.

How does laser hair removal work?

The laser's light is absorbed by melanin, the pigment in the hair shaft and follicle. The heat damages the follicle while the surrounding skin, which holds less pigment, is spared.

The lasers used are the alexandrite (755 nm), diode (800 to 810 nm) and long-pulsed Nd:YAG (1064 nm). Intense pulsed light (IPL) is a related, non-laser light source also used for hair removal.

Because the treatment targets pigment, it works best on dark hair. Cleveland Clinic notes it may not work as well on hair that is blonde, grey, white or red.

What is laser hair removal used for?

It reduces unwanted hair on the face and body: upper lip and chin, underarms, arms, legs, bikini line and back are common areas.

How well it works varies by area. A 2022 review of randomised trials found long-term reduction was greatest on the legs and lowest on the face, in line with how long hair takes to cycle in each area.

Who is laser hair removal suitable for?

The American Academy of Dermatology says it works best on light skin with dark hair. It is also widely used in darker skin, with the right laser.

That matters for many readers of this site. A review in the British Journal of Dermatology notes that most published laser data in non-white skin come from East Asian patients, including Korean, Japanese, Chinese and Thai, and that longer-wavelength lasers have been used safely in these skin types. Korean clinics treat Asian skin every day.

Who should avoid laser hair removal?

Tell the doctor before treatment if you have a tan, have used sunless tanner, or take any medicines. The AAD specifically asks patients to mention isotretinoin.

If your hair is very light, laser may do little. For darker skin, ask which laser is being used; our page on laser on darker skin covers this in more detail.

Laser hair removal devices and brand names in Korea

Korean clinics usually name the device on their price lists. Alexandrite, diode and Nd:YAG platforms are all commonly offered, sometimes as a combined device that carries two wavelengths.

In South Korea, laser devices that act on the hair follicle are medical devices. The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety requires them to carry a 의료기기 label and a permit number. You can ask the clinic which device it uses and look it up on the ministry's medical device portal.

Who performs it in Korea?

South Korea's Medical Service Act reserves medical acts for licensed medical personnel, and Korean courts have treated laser procedures as medical acts. In 2021, Chuncheon District Court fined a doctor and a nurse after the nurse performed laser underarm hair removal, holding it to be beyond nursing duties.

So in a Korean clinic, laser hair removal is a doctor's procedure. It is reasonable to ask who will hold the handpiece.

What happens during a session?

The area is shaved, protective eyewear is worn, and the laser is passed over the skin in pulses, usually with cooling. Small areas such as the upper lip take minutes; larger areas such as the legs take longer.

Cleveland Clinic advises shaving the area two to three days before, and not waxing, tweezing or plucking once treatment has started, because the laser needs the hair root in place.

How painful is laser hair removal?

Most people describe discomfort rather than pain, and cooling during treatment helps. The AAD notes that redness and swelling lasting one to three days are common afterwards.

Downtime and results timeline

There is usually no downtime. Cleveland Clinic says normal activities can resume right away. Avoid sun on the treated area, and use sunscreen; the Korean drug safety ministry advises sunscreen for at least a week after treatment.

Results build over the course. The US FDA defines "permanent hair reduction" as a stable drop in the number of hairs regrowing 6, 9 and 12 months after a treatment course, so long-term results are judged months after the last session.

Side effects and risks

Side effects are usually mild and short-lived. A Cochrane review found pain, redness, swelling, burned hairs and pigment changes were infrequently reported.

The main risk in darker skin is a change in skin colour. One review found pigment changes in up to 19% of patients with shorter-wavelength lasers in darker skin types, against 2 to 3% with Nd:YAG. Choosing the right laser for your skin type reduces that risk.

A small number of people grow more fine hair near the treated area, known as paradoxical hypertrichosis. A meta-analysis of 9,733 patients put this at 3%, mostly on the face and neck, and it often improved as treatment continued.

How many sessions are usually recommended?

Several, spaced weeks apart, because each session only affects hairs in their active growth phase. The AAD says most patients need 2 to 6 treatments every 4 to 6 weeks; Cleveland Clinic says six to eight treatments six to eight weeks apart.

This is why a single trip is a start. In a trial of 144 Asian patients treated with an alexandrite laser, one session reduced hair by 32% at nine months, two by 44% and three by 55%.

For a visitor, that leaves three reasonable plans:

  • One session as a start, continuing the course at home.
  • Sessions on repeat visits, if you travel to Korea every month or two.
  • A longer stay that fits two sessions four to six weeks apart.

What should foreigners ask a Korean clinic?

  • Which laser will be used, and is it suited to my skin type?
  • Who performs the treatment?
  • How many sessions do you expect for this area, and how far apart?
  • What is the price per session, and is a package refundable if I cannot return?
  • What settings were used, so a clinic at home can continue the course?

Packages of five or ten sessions are common on Korean price lists. If you cannot return within the interval, a per-session price is usually the better fit. See before you book for what to get in writing.

The bottom line

Laser hair removal heats the pigment in hair to slow regrowth, and works best on dark hair. It needs several sessions weeks apart, so a single trip to Korea gives a start: one session reduced hair by about a third in a trial in Asian skin. Korean clinics treat it as a doctor's procedure and commonly offer lasers suited to darker skin; ask which laser, how many sessions, and for your settings to take home.

Frequently asked questions

How many sessions will I need?
Several. The American Academy of Dermatology says most patients need 2 to 6 treatments, once every 4 to 6 weeks; Cleveland Clinic says six to eight, about six to eight weeks apart. The exact number depends on the body area, your hair and your skin.
Is laser hair removal permanent?
It gives long-term reduction rather than guaranteed removal. The US FDA's term is "permanent hair reduction", meaning a stable drop in the number of hairs regrowing at 6, 9 and 12 months after a course. Some fine hair usually remains, and maintenance sessions are common.
What can one session achieve?
Some lasting reduction. In a trial of 144 Asian patients treated with an alexandrite laser, one session reduced hair by 32% at nine months, two by 44% and three by 55%. A single session is worth having if you plan to continue the course, in Korea or at home.
Which laser is best for Asian skin?
Longer-wavelength lasers carry less risk of pigment change in darker skin. One review found pigment changes in up to 19% with shorter-wavelength lasers in darker skin types, against 2 to 3% with Nd:YAG. Much of the published data in non-white skin comes from East Asian patients, including Korean, Japanese and Chinese.
Why does it not work on my light-coloured hair?
The laser targets pigment in the hair. Cleveland Clinic notes that it may not work as well on hair that is blonde, grey, white or red, because there is little pigment for the laser to heat.
What should I do before my appointment?
Avoid tanning and sunless tanners, shave the area a few days before, and do not wax, pluck or tweeze. Tell the doctor about any medicines you take, especially if you have used isotretinoin.

Medical evidence

  1. Systematic review2006n = 444
    Laser and photoepilation for unwanted hair growth (Cochrane review)

    Short-term hair reduction of approximately 50% with alexandrite and diode lasers up to six months after treatment; long-term hair removal was not documented with any treatment.

  2. Systematic review2022n = 223
    Long-term hair reduction with laser and light: a systematic review of randomised controlled trials

    Greater long-term hair reduction was observed on body sites with longer hair growth cycles.

  3. Randomised trial2003n = 144
    Hair removal with the cooled long-pulsed alexandrite laser in Asian skin

    In subjects treated only one time, a 32% hair reduction was noted at 9 months after the single treatment.

  4. Systematic review2021n = 9733
    Paradoxical hypertrichosis after laser and light hair removal: a systematic review and meta-analysis

    Paradoxical hypertrichosis had a pooled prevalence of 3%, was associated with the face and neck, and in three of four studies gradually improved with continued therapy.
