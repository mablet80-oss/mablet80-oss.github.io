1e:T1f80,<h2 id="what-is-laser-hair-removal">What is laser hair removal?</h2>
<p>Laser hair removal uses a laser to heat the pigment in hair, damaging the follicle so that regrowth slows. It gives long-term <strong>reduction</strong> rather than guaranteed permanent removal.</p>
<p>In South Korea it is known as 레이저 제모, or simply 제모, and it is one of the most widely offered treatments in skin clinics.</p>
<h2 id="how-does-laser-hair-removal-work">How does laser hair removal work?</h2>
<p>The laser's light is absorbed by melanin, the pigment in the hair shaft and follicle. The heat damages the follicle while the surrounding skin, which holds less pigment, is spared.</p>
<p>The lasers used are the <strong>alexandrite</strong> (755 nm), <strong>diode</strong> (800 to 810 nm) and long-pulsed <strong>Nd:YAG</strong> (1064 nm). Intense pulsed light (IPL) is a related, non-laser light source also used for hair removal.</p>
<p>Because the treatment targets pigment, it works best on dark hair. Cleveland Clinic notes it may not work as well on hair that is blonde, grey, white or red.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-laser-hair-removal-used-for">What is laser hair removal used for?</h2>
<p>It reduces unwanted hair on the face and body: upper lip and chin, underarms, arms, legs, bikini line and back are common areas.</p>
<p>How well it works varies by area. A 2022 review of randomised trials found long-term reduction was greatest on the legs and lowest on the face, in line with how long hair takes to cycle in each area.</p>
<h2 id="who-is-laser-hair-removal-suitable-for">Who is laser hair removal suitable for?</h2>
<p>The American Academy of Dermatology says it works best on light skin with dark hair. It is also widely used in darker skin, with the right laser.</p>
<p>That matters for many readers of this site. A review in the British Journal of Dermatology notes that most published laser data in non-white skin come from <strong>East Asian patients</strong>, including Korean, Japanese, Chinese and Thai, and that longer-wavelength lasers have been used safely in these skin types. Korean clinics treat Asian skin every day.</p>
<h2 id="who-should-avoid-laser-hair-removal">Who should avoid laser hair removal?</h2>
<p>Tell the doctor before treatment if you have a tan, have used sunless tanner, or take any medicines. The AAD specifically asks patients to mention <strong>isotretinoin</strong>.</p>
<p>If your hair is very light, laser may do little. For darker skin, ask which laser is being used; our page on <a href="/concerns/laser-on-darker-skin/">laser on darker skin</a> covers this in more detail.</p>
<h2 id="laser-hair-removal-devices-and-brand-names-in-korea">Laser hair removal devices and brand names in Korea</h2>
<p>Korean clinics usually name the device on their price lists. Alexandrite, diode and Nd:YAG platforms are all commonly offered, sometimes as a combined device that carries two wavelengths.</p>
<p>In South Korea, laser devices that act on the hair follicle are <strong>medical devices</strong>. The Ministry of Food and Drug Safety requires them to carry a 의료기기 label and a permit number. You can ask the clinic which device it uses and look it up on the ministry's <a href="https://emedi.mfds.go.kr/">medical device portal</a>.</p>
<h2 id="who-performs-it-in-korea">Who performs it in Korea?</h2>
<p>South Korea's Medical Service Act reserves medical acts for licensed medical personnel, and Korean courts have treated laser procedures as medical acts. In 2021, Chuncheon District Court fined a doctor and a nurse after the nurse performed laser underarm hair removal, holding it to be beyond nursing duties.</p>
<p>So in a Korean clinic, laser hair removal is a doctor's procedure. It is reasonable to ask who will hold the handpiece.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-during-a-session">What happens during a session?</h2>
<p>The area is shaved, protective eyewear is worn, and the laser is passed over the skin in pulses, usually with cooling. Small areas such as the upper lip take minutes; larger areas such as the legs take longer.</p>
<p>Cleveland Clinic advises shaving the area two to three days before, and not waxing, tweezing or plucking once treatment has started, because the laser needs the hair root in place.</p>
<h2 id="how-painful-is-laser-hair-removal">How painful is laser hair removal?</h2>
<p>Most people describe discomfort rather than pain, and cooling during treatment helps. The AAD notes that redness and swelling lasting <strong>one to three days</strong> are common afterwards.</p>
<h2 id="downtime-and-results-timeline">Downtime and results timeline</h2>
<p>There is usually no downtime. Cleveland Clinic says normal activities can resume right away. Avoid sun on the treated area, and use sunscreen; the Korean drug safety ministry advises sunscreen for at least a week after treatment.</p>
<p>Results build over the course. The US FDA defines "permanent hair reduction" as a stable drop in the number of hairs regrowing <strong>6, 9 and 12 months</strong> after a treatment course, so long-term results are judged months after the last session.</p>
<h2 id="side-effects-and-risks">Side effects and risks</h2>
<p>Side effects are usually mild and short-lived. A Cochrane review found pain, redness, swelling, burned hairs and pigment changes were infrequently reported.</p>
<p>The main risk in darker skin is a change in skin colour. One review found pigment changes in up to 19% of patients with shorter-wavelength lasers in darker skin types, against 2 to 3% with Nd:YAG. Choosing the right laser for your skin type reduces that risk.</p>
<p>A small number of people grow more fine hair near the treated area, known as paradoxical hypertrichosis. A meta-analysis of 9,733 patients put this at 3%, mostly on the face and neck, and it often improved as treatment continued.</p>
<h2 id="how-many-sessions-are-usually-recommended">How many sessions are usually recommended?</h2>
<p>Several, spaced weeks apart, because each session only affects hairs in their active growth phase. The AAD says most patients need <strong>2 to 6 treatments every 4 to 6 weeks</strong>; Cleveland Clinic says <strong>six to eight treatments six to eight weeks apart</strong>.</p>
<p>This is why a single trip is a start. In a trial of 144 Asian patients treated with an alexandrite laser, one session reduced hair by <strong>32%</strong> at nine months, two by <strong>44%</strong> and three by <strong>55%</strong>.</p>
<p>For a visitor, that leaves three reasonable plans:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>One session as a start</strong>, continuing the course at home.</li>
<li><strong>Sessions on repeat visits</strong>, if you travel to Korea every month or two.</li>
<li><strong>A longer stay</strong> that fits two sessions four to six weeks apart.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="what-should-foreigners-ask-a-korean-clinic">What should foreigners ask a Korean clinic?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Which laser</strong> will be used, and is it suited to my skin type?</li>
<li><strong>Who performs</strong> the treatment?</li>
<li><strong>How many sessions</strong> do you expect for this area, and how far apart?</li>
<li><strong>What is the price per session</strong>, and is a package refundable if I cannot return?</li>
<li><strong>What settings were used</strong>, so a clinic at home can continue the course?</li>
</ul>
<p>Packages of five or ten sessions are common on Korean price lists. If you cannot return within the interval, a per-session price is usually the better fit. See <a href="/visit/before-you-book/">before you book</a> for what to get in writing.</p>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>Laser hair removal heats the pigment in hair to slow regrowth, and works best on dark hair. It needs several sessions weeks apart, so a single trip to Korea gives a start: one session reduced hair by about a third in a trial in Asian skin.
