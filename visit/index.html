Visit Guide

The journey rather than the procedure: what to settle before you book, whether anyone will speak English, paying, and getting home. What a treatment does is on its own page.

  • After you fly home: aftercare, follow-up, and going wrong at a distance

    Follow-up is the part of treating abroad that nobody plans and everybody needs. Leave South Korea holding the clinic's Korean name and contact, an itemised receipt, a record of exactly what was used on you and by whom, your consent forms, and written aftercare. If a complication becomes a dispute, South Korea has a statutory agency that mediates outside the courts.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • Booking a skin clinic in South Korea: what to settle first

    For self-pay skin treatment you generally need a passport, a consultation, and a way to pay — not residency, a Korean ID, or national health insurance. The larger questions are who will interpret, what is written down before you pay a deposit, and who you contact after you fly home. Settle those in writing rather than in conversation.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • How to look for a skin clinic in South Korea

    This site does not recommend clinics, so this is about method rather than names. Search where Koreans search rather than only in English, check the clinic against the register of institutions permitted to treat foreign patients, and know that a clinic's name carries a signal about its doctor's specialty. Any licensed doctor may legally perform these procedures — the question is whether you know which kind of doctor you have.

    Updated 2026-09-08
