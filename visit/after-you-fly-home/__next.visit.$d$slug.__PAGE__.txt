1f:T1367,<h2 id="the-gap-nobody-plans-for">The gap nobody plans for</h2>
<p>Treatment abroad has a structural problem that treatment at home does not. The person who treated
you is in another country, and the person who sees you next was not there, did not choose the
product, and has no record of what was done.</p>
<p>Almost everything below is about closing that gap before it opens, because the moment to collect
information is while you are still standing in the clinic.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-leave-south-korea-holding">What to leave South Korea holding</h2>
<p>Ask for these at the time. None of them are unusual requests in a clinic doing things properly.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The clinic's full Korean name and contact details.</strong> The Korean name is what any later search,
register lookup, or formal step will need.</li>
<li><strong>An itemised receipt.</strong> Not a total — the items.</li>
<li><strong>Exactly what was used on you.</strong> The product's Korean name, the dose or the device settings, and
<strong>who performed it</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Your signed consent forms</strong>, in a language you can read.</li>
<li><strong>Written aftercare</strong>, including which symptoms mean contact a doctor now rather than wait.</li>
</ul>
<p>That last item is the one most often left as spoken advice through an interpreter, which is to say
left as nothing.</p>
<h2 id="establish-the-contact-route-before-you-need-it">Establish the contact route before you need it</h2>
<p>Before you leave, ask three questions and note the answers:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Who handles a problem</strong> — the coordinator, the doctor, a general inbox?</li>
<li><strong>In what language?</strong></li>
<li><strong>How quickly do they reply?</strong></li>
</ol>
<p>"Message the coordinator on KakaoTalk, replies same day" is a plan. A vague answer is also
information, and it is much better to have it before you have paid than after something is not
settling.</p>
<p>While you are still in the country, the <strong>1330 helpline</strong> — 1330 locally, +82-2-1330 from abroad —
answers in eight languages and can interpret a call for you, which is useful when the clinic's
English-speaking coordinator is not on shift.</p>
<h2 id="when-you-are-home-and-something-is-not-right">When you are home and something is not right</h2>
<p>See a doctor where you live. That is not a lesser option; it is the correct one, and it is why the
record matters so much. A local doctor can act far faster when told the exact product, dose, and
date than when told "a filler, in Seoul, in March".</p>
<p>Give them the itemised record, the product names, and the written aftercare. If the treatment
involved an injectable, the specific product matters — some can be dissolved and others cannot, and
that decision is made faster with a name than with a description.</p>
<h2 id="if-it-becomes-a-dispute">If it becomes a dispute</h2>
<p>A complication is not automatically a dispute. But if it becomes one, South Korea has a statutory
route that does not require going to court.</p>
<p>The <strong>Korea Medical Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Agency</strong> mediates and arbitrates medical
disputes. The government's plain-language guidance frames eligibility as being a party to the
dispute or their representative, and <strong>states no nationality condition</strong>. An arbitration decision
carries the same effect as a final court judgment, which is why choosing arbitration is a decision
to be bound rather than a step to try.</p>
<p>The published material does not settle how proceedings run for someone who has already left the
country. If a problem has appeared and you are still in South Korea, that is an argument for
contacting the counselling centre before you fly rather than after.</p>
<p>Counselling centre: <strong>+82-2-1670-2545</strong> or <strong>+82-2-6210-0080</strong>, <strong><a href="mailto:kmedi@k-medi.or.kr">kmedi@k-medi.or.kr</a></strong>.</p>
<h2 id="why-the-registration-check-mattered">Why the registration check mattered</h2>
<p>An institution registered to treat foreign patients is required to carry <strong>medical malpractice
liability insurance</strong>. That single condition is what turns a claim from a moral argument into a
practical one.</p>
<p>It is also the reason this site keeps saying to confirm registration before paying a deposit. The
check takes minutes when you are choosing. It is worth very little once you need it.</p>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>Collect the record while you are still in the clinic: Korean name and contact, itemised receipt,
product and dose, who performed it, consent forms, written aftercare. Agree who you contact about a
problem, in what language, and how fast. See a local doctor if something goes wrong, and hand them
the specifics. And if it becomes a dispute, South Korea's statutory agency mediates outside the
