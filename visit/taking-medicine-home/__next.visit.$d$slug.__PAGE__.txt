20:T1292,<h2 id="the-rule-that-actually-governs">The rule that actually governs</h2>
<p>People ask this question the wrong way round. They ask what Korea will let them take out, when the
authority that decides is the one at the other end of the flight.</p>
<p>Korea is not generally where an ordinary personal quantity of medicine or skincare runs into
trouble. Your own country's health regulator is, because importing medicine is a regulated act in
most places and the traveller doing it is still an importer, just a small one.</p>
<p>So the practical question is not "can I take this out of Seoul". It is "how much of this may I
bring into the place I live", and that answer changes by passport.</p>
<h2 id="japan-worked-through-in-full">Japan, worked through in full</h2>
<p>Japan is worth doing properly, both because it is the largest source of visitors to Korean clinics
after China and because its health ministry publishes exact figures rather than a principle.</p>
<p>Without any import certificate, for personal use:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prescription drugs.</strong> Up to one month's supply.</li>
<li><strong>Drugs and quasi-drugs.</strong> Up to two months' supply.</li>
<li><strong>Vitamins.</strong> Up to four months' supply.</li>
<li><strong>External use items.</strong> Up to 24 pieces.</li>
<li><strong>Cosmetics.</strong> Up to 24 pieces per item.</li>
<li><strong>Medical devices.</strong> One set, for home use.</li>
</ul>
<p>Two of those deserve a second read.</p>
<p><strong>Cosmetics at 24 pieces per item</strong> is the line that catches people, because a Korean skincare haul
is a normal part of this trip. Note the wording: the cap is per item, not per suitcase. Twelve
different products in pairs is an easy pass. Twenty-five of the same sheet mask is not.</p>
<p><strong>External use at 24 pieces</strong> is where a clinic's medicated creams and ointments land, which
matters after any procedure that sends you home with topicals.</p>
<p>Above these figures you need an import certificate, applied for before you travel. It is a
document with a lead time, not a form you fill in at the airport, so it only helps people who
thought about it in advance.</p>
<h2 id="everywhere-else">Everywhere else</h2>
<p>Other destinations use the same shape with different numbers, so the reliable move is to read your
own regulator rather than a travel article.</p>
<p>For Australia, the Therapeutic Goods Administration publishes the traveller's exemption covering
quantity, documentation and packaging. For most other countries the equivalent body is the national
medicines or therapeutic goods regulator, and it is usually a single page on their site.</p>
<p>Whatever the figure turns out to be, three habits travel well. Keep things in original packaging
with the dispensing label attached. Carry the prescription or a letter. Do not carry medicine for
anybody else.</p>
<h2 id="controlled-substances-both-directions">Controlled substances, both directions</h2>
<p>This is a different category with a different process, and it rarely touches a skin clinic visit.</p>
<p>Narcotic and psychotropic medicines, which typically means opioids, benzodiazepines and stimulant
medicines for attention disorders, are controlled almost everywhere and move only with paperwork.
Korea requires ministry permission to bring such medicines into the country for personal treatment,
and that application is made weeks ahead rather than on the day of the flight.</p>
<p>The reason to mention it on a page about going home is that the person affected is usually carrying
their own regular medication into Korea, not leaving with something new.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-ask-the-clinic-for">What to ask the clinic for</h2>
<p>Everything above works better with documents, and documents are easiest to get while you are still
standing in the clinic.</p>
<p>Ask for the prescription itself, the product names written down rather than described, and where
you can, an English-language certificate. That last one is a defined item on Korea's national fee
schedule with its own ceiling rather than a favour you are requesting, and our page on
<a href="/official/medical-records-access/">your medical records and what they cost</a> covers how that works.</p>
<p>A border officer reading a document reaches a decision quickly. A border officer looking at
unlabelled boxes reaches a slower one.</p>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line">The bottom line</h2>
<p>Leaving Korea is seldom the constraint. Arriving home can be, and the numbers that bind are
published by your own regulator.</p>
