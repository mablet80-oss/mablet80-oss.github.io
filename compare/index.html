Comparisons

Two treatments, the same criteria, no universal winner. How they work, who they suit, downtime, results, and cost side by side.

  • Rejuran vs Juvelook: two different bets on your own collagen

    Both are injected to change skin quality rather than to add contour, and both work through your own tissue response rather than by filling space. Rejuran's active is polynucleotide, purified from salmon; Juvelook's is poly-D,L-lactic acid, a collagen stimulator. The differences that matter to a patient are reversibility and the state of the evidence, and they do not point the same way.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • Thermage vs Ultherapy: heat spread wide, or focused deep

    Neither is better in the abstract; they heat different things in different ways. Thermage is monopolar radiofrequency, spreading heat through a large volume of tissue up to around 20 mm deep. Ultherapy is microfocused ultrasound, delivered at set depths, the deepest reaching the SMAS — the layer a surgeon pulls on. Both tighten skin that is still in place, and neither repositions tissue that has descended.

    Updated 2026-09-08

  • Ultherapy vs Shurink: what the records show, and what no study shows

    Both deliver focused ultrasound to heat tissue at set depths, and in a Korean clinic the two names are often offered as substitutes for each other. One documented difference separates them: Ultherapy carries real-time imaging that lets the operator see the layers being treated, and it is the only microfocused ultrasound device with United States clearance for visualisation as well as treatment. What does not exist is a head-to-head trial, so any clinic claim that one outperforms the other is an opinion rather than a finding.

    Updated 2026-09-10
