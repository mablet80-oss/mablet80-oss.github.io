22:T131b,<h2 id="what-they-have-in-common">What they have in common</h2>
<p>Both are injected across an area rather than placed in one spot, both are aimed at <strong>skin quality</strong>
rather than contour, and both work by provoking your own tissue rather than by occupying space.</p>
<p>That last point is the one that matters and the one marketing blurs. Neither is a filler. If the
complaint is a hollow or descended tissue, neither is the tool, however they are sold.</p>
<p>Both are also sold as <strong>courses</strong>, so the number to ask for is the total rather than the price of a
session.</p>

<h2 id="neither-can-be-undone">Neither can be undone</h2>
<p>This is the practical warning both pages carry and it is worth stating once more against each other,
because the reassurance patients have about hyaluronic acid filler does not transfer.</p>
<p><strong>Hyaluronidase dissolves hyaluronic acid.</strong> It does not remove polynucleotide, and it does not
remove PDLLA. In the PDLLA trial, one participant's moderate swelling was treated with an
intralesional steroid and hyaluronidase — and that hyaluronidase was acting on the <strong>hyaluronic acid
component</strong> of the product, not on the polymer.</p>
<p>So with either, a conservative first treatment is not caution for its own sake. It is the only exit
available.</p>
<h2 id="the-evidence-honestly-on-both-sides">The evidence, honestly, on both sides</h2>
<p>Neither is well evidenced. They are thin differently, which is the useful part.</p>
<p><strong>Polynucleotides</strong> have volume: a 2024 systematic review pooled <strong>nine studies covering 219
patients</strong>. But the studies were rated <strong>low to moderate quality</strong>, none scored fully on critical
appraisal, and of the six reporting wrinkle improvement, <strong>half reached statistical significance</strong>.
The reviewers' conclusion asks for rigorous studies.</p>
<p><strong>PDLLA</strong> has one better-designed study and less of it: a 2026 <strong>split-face trial in 33 subjects</strong>,
each person acting as their own control, finding the PDLLA product <strong>non-inferior to PLLA</strong> —
wrinkle severity improvement of −1.37 against −1.20 at week 24, no significant difference. Its most
telling figure is that <strong>70% of patients had no preference between the two sides of their own face</strong>.
The authors say directly that long-term safety needs further study.</p>
<p>So: more studies of weaker quality against fewer studies of better design, both small. Nothing here
establishes either as superior, and anybody who tells you otherwise is not citing this.</p>
<h2 id="what-that-means-when-a-clinic-proposes-one">What that means when a clinic proposes one</h2>
<p>The decision is not going to be settled by evidence, because the evidence does not separate them.
What you can do instead:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ask <strong>which product exactly</strong>, in Korean, and check which MFDS portal it is licensed on.</li>
<li>Ask <strong>how many sessions and the total</strong>, since both are courses.</li>
<li>Ask <strong>when to judge it</strong> — and hold the clinic to that date rather than an earlier one.</li>
<li>Treat any precise percentage claim as marketing.</li>
<li>Remember neither can be dissolved, and start conservatively.</li>
</ul>
<p>If both are proposed together, ask what each is for. No study examines them in combination, so a
claim about what the pair achieves is not coming from the literature.</p>
