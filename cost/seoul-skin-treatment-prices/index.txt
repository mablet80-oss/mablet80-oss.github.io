29:T172f,<h2 id="seoul-prices-at-a-glance-november-2026">Seoul prices at a glance, November 2026</h2>
<p>The table above gives one range for each treatment, built from the posted prices of 10 to 15 Seoul clinics for the same product at the same dose. Every figure includes VAT. The median, the price half the clinics were at or below, is in the note column and is a better guide to a typical price than either end of the range.</p>
<p>Rejuran has its own page with the same method: <a href="/cost/rejuran-cost-korea/">Rejuran price in Seoul</a>.</p>
<h2 id="botox-the-brand-sets-the-price">Botox: the brand sets the price</h2>
<p>For 50 units of jaw botox, the dose these listings most often quote for the masseter, Korean brands cost ₩18,600 to ₩53,900 and Xeomin or Allergan cost ₩86,900 to ₩148,500. The medians were ₩38,500 and ₩119,900.</p>
<p>The same clinic usually lists several brands side by side, so the brand is the first thing to check on a quote. Some of the lowest Korean-brand prices were for split vials, and some clinics charge more to open a new vial in front of you. The <a href="/treatments/botox/">botox page</a> explains the Korean brands and how they are licensed.</p>
<h2 id="filler-and-juvelook-priced-per-cc">Filler and Juvelook: priced per cc</h2>
<p>Hyaluronic acid filler is priced per cc. Korean brands such as Neuramis, Chaeum and YVOIRE cost ₩59,000 to ₩319,000 per cc, with a median of ₩104,500. Restylane, Juvéderm and Belotero cost ₩220,000 to ₩495,000, with a median of ₩275,000.</p>
<p>Juvelook, a collagen stimulator rather than a filler, cost ₩44,000 to ₩143,000 per cc, with a median of ₩55,000. Clinics price Juvelook Skin and Juvelook Volume the same or close to it, so the range covers both. See <a href="/treatments/dermal-filler/">dermal filler</a> and <a href="/treatments/juvelook/">Juvelook</a> for what each one does.</p>
<h2 id="lifting-devices-ultherapy-thermage-and-shurink">Lifting devices: Ultherapy, Thermage and Shurink</h2>
<p>These are priced by the number of shots.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ultherapy, 300 shots:</strong> ₩600,000 to ₩1,320,000, median ₩945,000. Most clinics now list Ultherapy Prime.</li>
<li><strong>Thermage FLX, 600 shots:</strong> ₩1,490,000 to ₩2,409,000, median ₩2,194,500.</li>
<li><strong>Shurink Universe, 300 shots:</strong> ₩86,900 to ₩165,000, median ₩98,450.</li>
</ul>
<p>Shurink and Ultherapy both use focused ultrasound, and their prices differ by roughly ten times. <a href="/compare/ultherapy-vs-shurink/">Ultherapy vs Shurink</a> covers what is known about the difference. Shot counts are not comparable between devices, so compare Ultherapy with Ultherapy and Thermage with Thermage.</p>
<h2 id="potenza-check-the-tip">Potenza: check the tip</h2>
<p>Potenza, a microneedle radiofrequency device, cost ₩122,200 to ₩209,000 for the full face with the tip included, median ₩162,000. Clinics name the tip they use, and some listings bundle soothing care. The <a href="/treatments/potenza/">Potenza page</a> covers what it treats.</p>
<h2 id="skin-boosters-and-toning">Skin boosters and toning</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Skinvive, 1 cc:</strong> ₩180,400 to ₩400,000, median ₩297,000. Clinics also sell generic hyaluronic acid skin boosters, often called 물광주사, at much lower prices, but under many different products and volumes, so they are not ranged here. The <a href="/treatments/skin-booster/">skin booster page</a> explains the category.</li>
<li><strong>Pico toning, one session:</strong> ₩19,000 to ₩99,000, median ₩64,900. Listings name PicoSure, PicoPlus or another device where they name one.</li>
<li><strong>Laser toning, one session:</strong> ₩15,000 to ₩80,000, median ₩50,000.</li>
</ul>
<p>Toning is usually done as a course, and several clinics list 5- or 10-session packages at a lower price per session. The ranges above are for a single session. For what each laser does for pigmentation, see <a href="/treatments/pico-laser/">pico laser</a> and <a href="/treatments/laser-toning/">laser toning</a>.</p>
<h2 id="why-the-same-treatment-varies">Why the same treatment varies</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Offer type.</strong> Most prices in Seoul are promotional listings on a booking platform, many with an end date. The crossed-out figure beside them is not what anyone pays; the listed price is.</li>
<li><strong>First visits.</strong> Many clinics offer a one-time first-visit price below their usual one. These are left out of the ranges.</li>
<li><strong>VAT.</strong> Platform prices include VAT; clinic websites often show prices before VAT, marked 부가세 별도.</li>
<li><strong>What is bundled.</strong> Some prices include a skin analysis, a soothing mask or sedation.</li>
</ul>
<p>Korean law requires clinics to post prices for non-covered treatment, which includes all aesthetic treatment, and bars them from charging more than the posted price for the same item. Keep a screenshot of the listing you book from.</p>
<h2 id="how-the-prices-were-collected">How the prices were collected</h2>
<p>On 3 November 2026 we read each price on the clinic's own listing on 강남언니 (Gangnam Unni), the booking platform most Seoul clinics use to post prices, or on the clinic's own website. We kept one price per clinic for each product and dose, the lowest it offered to everyone. The dataset of 295 prices from 125 clinics is linked below, with the page each price came from. We do not name clinics on this page and do not recommend any of them.</p>
